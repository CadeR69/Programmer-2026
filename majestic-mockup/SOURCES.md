Nothing on the mockup is invented. Every dish name, description and claim is
traceable to a source listed here, so you can defend any line of it in the meeting.

## The two things to lead with

**1. They do not have a website. Their listed website is a Facebook page.**

Their business listing points at `facebook.com/Majestic-Pizza-Steak-House-1172485…`
That is the exact case your own site names: *"Just a phone number and a Facebook
page — while the business down the road shows up first on Google."* You wrote that
bucket before you knew it described them.

**2. `majesticmayfield.com` exists and now serves an online-gambling page.**

- The root of that domain serves an Indonesian slots site ("MAWARTOTO / Slot88"),
  verified 7 Sept 2026.
- `majesticmayfield.com/Home` returns 404, but search engines still hold a cached
  copy of it showing **their restaurant's hours** — so that domain was theirs at
  some point and is not any more.

Check it on your phone before you walk in. Don't assume they know. It is also the
cleanest possible argument for Section 7 of your agreement — the domain goes in the
client's name, so a lapse can't hand their address to someone else.

## Deliberately blank

**All prices.** Three price data points existed, all from old reviews, none current.
Putting guessed numbers on a restaurant's own site is the fastest way to look like
you don't know their business. The page says so plainly and asks for their menu —
a better conversation than a wrong number, and a reason to follow up.
